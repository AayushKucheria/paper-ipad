The error inside a trapezoid

A short derivation you can work through on the page

A straight line makes an easy substitute for a curve. Between two points a distance h apart, it turns the area under the curve into a trapezoid. The estimate is the average of the two endpoint heights times the width.

Try the simplest curved example, f(x) = x² on the interval from 0 to h. The trapezoid gives h³/2. The integral gives h³/3. Sketch the curve and the straight line, then see if you can find the area between them.

Trapezoid − integral = h³/6

That h³ matters. Cut the width in half and the error on each small interval falls by a factor of eight for this quadratic. Across the same total width you now have twice as many intervals, so the total error falls by a factor of four.
